Ordering and Transportation of Raw Materials for Production Enterprises

The problem: a plant that makes 28,200 m³ of product a week from three raw materials must keep at least two weeks of stock. It orders from 402 suppliers and ships through 8 transporters, each carrying up to 6,000 m³ a week. Past records show how much each supplier delivered, not how much it could have delivered, so the plan depends on how that history is read. The plan below uses the fewest suppliers that meet the stock rule, with its stock simulated 2,000 times. Change the settings below to see how the answer depends on supplier capacity and safety stock.

Findings

Past supply rarely covered production. Deliveries made enough for 28,200 m³ in only 28% of the 240 weeks. Large deliveries repeat every 24 weeks, and orders far above a supplier's usual amount were simply not filled.

The capacity assumption drives every answer. Reading each supplier's history more cautiously raises the number of suppliers needed from 14 to 23 to 36, and lowers the most the plant could produce from 40,889 to 34,396 to 30,245 m³ a week. If suppliers can only repeat their typical deliveries, 28,200 cannot be sustained at all.

A little safety stock buys a lot of reliability. Under the default assumption, the 23-supplier plan never stops production in simulation, but keeps the full two-week buffer in only 78% of runs. Planning a quarter-week of extra stock raises that to 95% with the same suppliers.

Why not the most optimistic reading? It looks safe in simulation, because the simulation does not vary capacity. But it counts one-off deliveries as capacity. One supplier delivered 30,977 m³ once and nothing at all in 7 of its 10 cycles.

Method: suppliers ranked with entropy-weighted TOPSIS; the fewest suppliers found as a 0/1 covering problem among the top 60; cheapest orders and least-loss transport from linear programs; stock simulated 2,000 times per plan. All rules (capacity, 48,000 m³ weekly transport, two-week stock) are checked automatically in the project repository.
Data: attachments to the 2021 China Undergraduate Mathematical Contest in Modeling, Problem C.
