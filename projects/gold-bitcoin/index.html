2022 MCM Problem C · Honorable Mention · reproducible analysis

Pick a risk level and see what five years of daily trading would have returned

Start with $1,000 on 9/11/2016. Every day the strategy looks only at prices up to that day and decides whether to buy, hold or sell gold and bitcoin, paying 1% per gold trade and 2% per bitcoin trade. Gold trades only on days the London market sets a price. Change the settings below and the full 1,826-day backtest reruns in your browser.

What the analysis found

The strategy did not beat buying and holding bitcoin. Bitcoin rose about 75-fold in these five years. Holding it turned $1,000 into $73,127, but at one point the position was down 83% from its peak. At the default settings the strategy ended at $21,064 with a worst drop of 46% and almost the same return per unit of risk (Sharpe ratio 1.50 vs 1.49).

Risk tolerance and the bitcoin limit trade final value for smaller losses. Every step toward caution lowers both the final value and the worst drop. The map above shows the trade-off.

A trader who knew every future price could have reached about $33.7 billion, which is why very large reported returns usually mean future prices leaked into the decisions.

Method: each day, expected returns are exponentially weighted averages of past returns and risk is an exponentially weighted covariance. The strategy picks weights that maximize horizon × expected return − ½ × risk aversion × horizon × variance − commissions, with no short selling or borrowing, solved exactly for each buy/sell direction. Momentum half-life and holding horizon are fixed at 30 days. This method was chosen over an LSTM return forecast trained only on past data, which did worse; the comparison is in the project repository. The browser engine is a port of the project's R code and matches its results to within 1 part in 1013.
